1. Customer Support Performance & Churn

“What makes customers unhappy, and can tickets warn us before they leave?”

Two years of synthetic helpdesk data for a made-up company: 17,000+ tickets, 3,200 customers, with an outage and a price increase hidden inside. Could my analysis find them?

low CSAT score, by SLA        (1 # = 2%)
on time  |###                    5.5%
missed   |##############        28.9%

90-day churn, price increase
before   |####                   8.6%
after    |#######               13.9%
  • Speed drives satisfaction. Urgent tickets wait longest: 34.2% missed the SLA.
  • CSAT can hide churn. Billing tickets almost doubled after the price rise.
  • Logistic regression flags 61.7% of leavers (ROC-AUC 0.70).
  • 26% of customers churned. 62% of them never said why.
read the full story

Problem: most cancelling customers give no reason. Tickets are an early signal already collected daily.

Do: answer Urgent tickets first in incidents. Treat post-price-change Billing tickets as a churn warning. Use the model for a check-in list.

Limits: synthetic data is cleaner than real life. Only 39.5% of tickets have CSAT. Before/after shows a link, not cause.

2. Customer Segmentation & Lifetime Value

“Who are the most valuable customers, and what will they spend next?”

A UK retailer with 375K transactions treated every customer the same. I built RFM metrics, K-Means segments, a Random Forest spend model and a four-page Power BI dashboard.

  • My first CLV model leaked data. I found it and fixed it.

3. Menstrual Cycle & Workplace Productivity

“Does pain affect how much work someone can do, and can we predict it?”

17,972 logs from 2,000 users, compared with sleep, exercise, diet and stress. Random Forest model plus a five-page Looker Studio dashboard.

  • Pain is the main driver of lost hours: 3.4 hrs/day in high-pain phases.
  • 51.7% of users report PMS. It’s the majority, not a niche.
  • Model accuracy 75%. Flexible high-pain days beat generic wellness.
